The games you won and never looked at

Almost everyone reviews their losses. But a mistake your opponent failed to punish is still a mistake — and it is invisible, because the game ended fine. This looks at your recent games and finds the serious ones nobody made you pay for.

How this decides what counts as a mistake

Every position is evaluated by Stockfish 10, running in your browser. Nothing is uploaded; the only network request is to Chess.com's public games API.

  • A serious mistake is a move that loses 300 centipawns or more — about the value of a knight — against the engine's view of the position before it.
  • Evaluations are clamped to ±10 pawns before comparing, so going from completely winning to merely winning is not counted as a blunder.
  • Unpunished means that two plies later the position had returned to within 150 centipawns of where it stood before the mistake. Your opponent did not take what you gave them.
  • Opening is the first 12 moves. Endgame begins when the non-pawn material left on the board — queens 9, rooks 5, minors 3 — falls to 20 or less. Everything between is the middlegame.

These thresholds are conventions, not laws. They are written here so you can disagree with them.

What this cannot tell you
  • It reads no clock. A mistake in time trouble looks identical to one made with ten minutes left, so "endgame" and "low on time" are confounded in any result you see here.
  • At depth 12 the engine is far stronger than most of us, but it is not perfect, and shallow evaluations of quiet positions can wobble.
  • Each position is its own search, and the one after your move looks a ply deeper than the one before it. When tactics sit just past that horizon, the two disagree and the gap gets charged to your move. Moves the engine itself would have played are dropped for exactly this reason, but the same wobble survives in smaller amounts elsewhere. Deeper settings shrink it.
  • A small sample of games is a small sample. Ten games is a hint, not a diagnosis.
